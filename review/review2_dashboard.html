The layer, not the algorithm, was the bottleneck: no block-4 configuration passed 23 hits; block 3 reached 33. The strict winner (block 3, LayerCAM, smoothing, 320 px) makes 5 %-of-image blobs, IoU 0.166, and costs 6× the compute; the adopted runner-up is 2 hits behind with the best IoU of all 111. Three briefings, same films: what changes per report

Means over 200 reports per condition. Unsupported = asserted but not flagged by the classifier; false = not in the NIH labels.

C · grounded (deployed in the app)A · image only, B · image + scores

Handing the model raw scores (B) makes it assert 2.9× more findings than the image alone and never leaves a normal film clean. Adding the heatmap zone (C) pulls it back toward the labels, not merely toward the classifier: fewer unsupported and fewer false findings at almost the same recall. Only ~5 % of what MedGemma adds beyond the classifier is in the labels. No condition produces a draft fit to go out unread.

Towards review 3

Last week of September 2026. Three working weeks from this review. Everything marked planned is scoped, has a script or notebook to start from and a measured baseline to beat; stretch items run if the first three land early.

What review 3 will show

  1. The heatmap method confirmed (or revised) on all 984 NIH radiologist boxes, not 53. Removes the small-sample caveat on section 02.
  2. A 320 px full-data classifier beside the current 224 px one on the official test split, with an hflip ablation. Targets Nodule, Mass, Pneumonia.
  3. Calibrated probabilities, so the app's scores read as probabilities and thresholds are principled.
  4. A second grounded briefing, D, that gives MedGemma the heatmap image, re-scored on the same 200 films.
  5. A small blinded reader study of grounded vs ungrounded drafts, if time allows.
  6. The live app, the updated report and the repository, unchanged in scope, sharper in numbers.
